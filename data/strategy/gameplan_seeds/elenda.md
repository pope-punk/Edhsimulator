Elenda, Saint of Dusk

Standing Plan — how to play this deck:

Use this document as durable deck doctrine, not as a turn script. At planning checkpoints, reread the supplied seed, inspect the current long- and short-term plans, and use `inspect deck` to search beyond cards already seen. Use `inspect roles` and focused role queries—especially `wincon`, `lifegain`, `lifegain_payoff`, `engine`, `ramp`, `tutor`, `removal`, `board_wipe`, `protection`, `recursion`, and `token_production`—to recover the private strategic notes attached to relevant cards; inspect named cards and packages before committing to a line. The high-level job is to place the right payoff before the appropriate lifegain pattern, then convert life into commander pressure, Sorin damage, distributed drain, or resilient value while retaining a recovery route. Choose actions by asking what advances a supported win route, which payoff or resource is missing, what removal or reset must be respected, and whether recursion should precede graveyard restriction. Let the short-term plan hold immediate thresholds and sequencing; revise the long-term plan only when the match-level route, resource posture, threat model, or recovery plan actually changes.

Keep the three planning layers distinct. This Standing Plan is immutable, reusable guidance for how to pilot the deck: its mechanics, win conditions, decision principles, inspection vocabulary, and recovery doctrine. The private Long-Term Plan is the determinate objective for this particular match: the current primary win route or development goal, posture toward every opponent, plausible future branches, and observable cues for pivoting. The private Short-Term Plan is the executable bridge between current reality and that goal: synthesize game status, inspections, and recent rationales into direct next actions, with explicit sequencing, priority and trigger timing, attacks, blocks, targets, interaction to hold, and hazards to avoid.
Deck overview:


Treat Glasswing Grace as the tapped 34th land when necessary, and keep two-land hands only with Wayfarer’s Bauble or a dependable rock. The early decision is whether to accelerate to four mana or establish Sorin of House Markov, Dawn of Hope, Ajani’s Pridemate, Essence Channeler, or High Priest of Penance. The deck contains two distinct categories of lifegain payoff. Pridemate, Elenda’s Hierophant, Essence Channeler, Fiendish Panda, Twinblade Paladin, Marauding Blight-Priest, Exemplar of Light, and Dawn of Hope prefer numerous separate gain events; Defiant Bloodlord, Midnight Snack, and transformed Sorin prefer one enormous event. Play the payoff before the gain. Angel of Vitality and Leyline of Hope each add one to the amount gained but do not create additional events, whereas extort triggers, separate lifelink damage steps, Polluted Bonds, Parasitic Impetus, Prayer of Binding, and Pristine Talisman produce independently useful triggers. Exemplar draws only once each turn, so Pristine Talisman can be tapped during another player’s turn or Mikaeus can place a direct counter then to earn an additional card. Pontiff of Blight is a hidden mass-drain engine: every other creature supplies another extort trigger to the next spell, making Dawn of Hope Soldiers, Angel of Invention Servos, Midnight Snack Foods, and incidental bodies strategically valuable; Pontiff cannot extort its own casting, so retain a cheap spell for afterward. Trading Post is the other overlooked engine. It can discard Rune-Scarred Demon or Noxious Gearhulk to gain four before reanimating it, sacrifice a Servo or Food for a card, sacrifice Solemn Simulacrum for two cards, or cash in a Goat, Hierophant, Essence Channeler, or grown Fiendish Panda to recover Celestial Armor, Gearhulk, Solemn, or another artifact while still receiving the creature’s death trigger.

The primary combat thresholds are 41 life, where Elenda becomes a 5/5 with menace, and 50, where she becomes a 10/10; Leyline separately strengthens the entire board at 47. Restless Fortress’s attack trigger resolves before blockers and can push Elenda over one of those thresholds mid-combat. Arvad, Angelic Destiny, Celestial Armor, Swiftfoot Boots, Rogue’s Passage, and the anthem effects create a compact commander-damage plan, while Twinblade Paladin carrying Give In to Violence or Glasswing Grace produces two lifelink damage steps. The drain plan is more explosive: cast Sorin of House Markov before gaining at least three life in the precombat portion of the turn, enter the postcombat main phase to transform him, then use −1 for the total amount gained. Exsanguinate or Debt to the Deathless can simultaneously fuel that activation, Defiant Bloodlord, and Midnight Snack. If Sorin, Vengeful Bloodlord is also present, the transformed planeswalker’s damage has lifelink, generating another enormous gain for the remaining payoffs. Bank Lotus Blossom for precisely this sort of protected X-spell or double-spell turn. For resilience, temporary power matters when sacrificing creatures to Trading Post: Hierophant creates tokens using its last known power, and Fiendish Panda can reanimate a creature with mana value up to its last known power, allowing a combat trick or Aura to expand the target range. Essence Channeler transfers its accumulated counters, Solemn replaces itself, and Angelic Destiny returns after a death. High Priest plus Valorous Stance or Celestial Armor turns combat damage into repeatable nonland removal. Kunoros should enter only after using the desired recursion because it also disables Zombify, Restart Sequence, kicked Nullpriest, Fiendish Panda, and Sorin. Akroma’s Vengeance is strongest as a planned reset with a planeswalker remaining; cash in artifacts first, sweep, and rebuild through Sorin or reanimation. Rune-Scarred Demon and Diabolic Tutor should therefore find the missing role—mana, draw, protection, removal, evasion, reset, or payoff—rather than reflexively taking the largest drain spell.





Standing Plan Summary (reusable heuristics):

- Establish reliable white and black mana, then place the relevant payoff before spending lifegain events. Decide whether the current hand rewards many small gains or one large gain.
- Track the 41-, 47-, and 50-life thresholds and identify whether the cleanest route is commander damage, a large Sorin conversion, distributed drain, or resilient value.
- Reserve Lotus Blossom and other burst mana for a protected X-spell or double-spell turn; keep cheap spells available when Pontiff of Blight can multiply extort triggers.
- Sequence recursion before Kunoros, cash in replaceable artifacts or creatures before a reset, and use temporary power to improve death and reanimation payoffs when relevant.
- Reassess after major lifegain, a payoff entering or leaving, a tutor, or a board wipe. Record the chosen win route, next threshold, missing role, and interaction to preserve as dynamic updates.
- Aggressively use table talk to instigate conflict among opponents: emphasize public threats, encourage opponents to spend attacks and interaction on one another, and exploit those exchanges without revealing hidden information or presenting unsupported claims as fact.
