Omo, Queen of Vesuva

Standing Plan — how to play this deck:

Use this document as durable deck doctrine, not as a turn script. At planning checkpoints, reread the supplied seed, inspect the current long- and short-term plans, and use `inspect deck` to search beyond cards already seen. Use `inspect roles` and focused role queries—especially `wincon`, `land_synergy`, `engine`, `ramp`, `tutor`, `removal`, `protection`, `token_production`, and `card_advantage`—to recover the private strategic notes attached to relevant cards; inspect named cards and packages before committing to a line. The high-level job is to establish functional colors and high-output lands, distribute everything counters intelligently, and convert that infrastructure into a supported Dark Depths, Maze's End, landfall-token, or animated-land finish without losing the utility needed to recover. Choose actions by asking what advances the selected finish, which land or engine role is missing, what interaction constrains the line, and which alternate finish remains credible. Let the short-term plan hold immediate land sequencing and activations; revise the long-term plan only when the match-level route, resource posture, threat model, or recovery plan actually changes.

Keep the three planning layers distinct. This Standing Plan is immutable, reusable guidance for how to pilot the deck: its mechanics, win conditions, decision principles, inspection vocabulary, and recovery doctrine. The private Long-Term Plan is the determinate objective for this particular match: the current primary win route or development goal, posture toward every opponent, plausible future branches, and observable cues for pivoting. The private Short-Term Plan is the executable bridge between current reality and that goal: synthesize game status, inspections, and recent rationales into direct next actions, with explicit sequencing, priority and trigger timing, attacks, blocks, targets, interaction to hold, and hazards to avoid.
Deck Overview:


Mulligan for a functional mana sequence rather than a nominal land count: many of the 40 lands are tapped or colorless, so keep two only with Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Expedition Map, Growth Spiral, or a convincing route to the third land; Crop Rotation is not an additional land because the sacrifice is an extra cost. Put tapped lands down on otherwise idle turns, resolve Dryad, Azusa, or Oracle before using the additional land drops they grant, and cast Spelunking before Cloudpost, Maze’s End, Vesuva, Hour of Promise, or other tapped-land sequences. Omo is normally the turn-three play. Place everything counters on durable, differently named lands: each marked land gains all five basic types for fixing, plus Gate, Locus, Desert, Cave, and every Urza subtype. A marked Urza’s Tower, Mine, or Power-Plant consequently satisfies its own subtype conditions, while marked lands enlarge Cloudpost, Baldur’s Gate, Basilisk Gate, Desert Warfare, and Sage of the Maze. Planar Nexus already possesses every nonbasic type in every zone and can be found by Maze’s End. Spread counters rather than duplicating them, and mark Omo or other nonland creatures to make them Merfolk for Hakbal, Elves for Priest of Titania, and Illusions protected by Lord of the Unreal. When Omo and Aven Courier attack together, place Courier’s trigger below Omo’s so Omo creates a new everything counter before Courier chooses which counter to copy. Land tutors should address the present bottleneck: fixing when colors are weak, Cloudpost or a printed Urza land when type support is established, Talon Gates of Madara for an instant-speed phase-out, or the missing half of a specific victory line. Xolatoyac turns marked lands into a second pool of end-step mana, Magus of the Candelabra profits whenever individual lands tap for more than one, Mana Reflection doubles every such engine, and Sage of the Maze converts spare Gates into repeated two-mana activations.

The cleanest compact finish is Dark Depths copied after entry by Thespian’s Stage or Mirage Mirror, preferably during the final end step before attacking. Vesuva and Copy Land enter as copies and therefore acquire ten ice counters, so they cannot perform the shortcut. Maze’s End instead requires ten differently named Gates after Maze has returned itself to hand as part of the activation cost; duplicate basic lands and permanents copying the same land name do not improve that count. Sunken Palace’s special mana can copy a Maze activation, Hour of Promise, Drown in Dreams, Finale of Revelation, or a generic-cost land-tutor activation such as Expedition Map or Urza’s Cave, but exiling seven cards competes with Uro, Ramunap Excavator, and Finale’s graveyard use. The creature plans are much broader than they first appear. Tatyova, Scute Swarm, Rampaging Baloths, Avenger of Zendikar, and Desert Warfare should enter before the extra land drops; Simic Growth Chamber and Wonderscape Sage recycle lands for another trigger, with Sage avoiding its discard when the returned land was marked. Desert Warfare makes sacrificing a marked land temporary, returning Volatile Fault, Blast Zone, Horizon of Progress, Urza’s Cave, Hidden Nursery, or another utility land at the next end step. Sage of the Maze can animate several lands, and a sorcery-speed Basilisk Gate activation can enlarge Jyoti before combat so Jyoti gives the animated army a much larger bonus. March from Velis Vel can turn every marked land into a hasty copy of Apex Devastator or another large nonlegendary creature; copying a legend risks losing the land army to the legend rule. Helm of the Host on Omo produces one entry trigger and two attack triggers that combat. Aggressive Biomancy is especially potent with Acidic Slime, Ulvenwald Hydra, or Terastodon, while Replication Technique can multiply Cloudpost, Mana Reflection, or a landfall engine. Whelming Wave preserves marked nonland creatures because they are Serpents, but not animated lands, and Omo must remain present; Evacuation can instead return Omo directly to hand, avoiding commander tax. Before casting Finale for ten or more, replay the graveyard lands and escape Uro that would otherwise be shuffled away.




Standing Plan Summary (reusable heuristics):

- Keep and sequence for functional colored mana, not nominal land count. Resolve effects that improve tapped lands or grant extra land plays before spending the land drops they enhance.
- Cast Omo on schedule when practical and spread everything counters across durable, differently named lands; place creature counters only when the type synergies or protection materially matter.
- Let each tutor solve the present bottleneck: colors, high-output mana, utility interaction, or a specific supported finish. Do not assemble a win condition while leaving the mana engine unable to execute it.
- Choose deliberately among Dark Depths, Maze's End, landfall tokens, and animated-land combat, while preserving graveyard lands and flexible utility where they support the chosen route.
- Reassess after tutors, copied lands, new counter placement, or a major reset. Record the active finish, missing land/engine, and interaction to hold around as dynamic updates.
- Aggressively use table talk to instigate conflict among opponents: emphasize public threats, encourage opponents to spend attacks and interaction on one another, and exploit those exchanges without revealing hidden information or presenting unsupported claims as fact.
