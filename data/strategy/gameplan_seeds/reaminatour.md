Aminatou, Veil Piercer

Use this document as durable deck doctrine, not as a turn script. Initialize roles, deck and seed once per logical seat/game, using the supplied seed and retained knowledge inventory. Reuse the full deck index and roles to search beyond cards already seen; a context checkpoint is not a fresh initialization. Read the current plans and use focused role queries—especially `wincon`, `combo_piece`, `engine`, `recursion`, `sacrifice_outlet`, `tutor`, `removal`, `counterspell`, `protection`, `card_advantage`, and `card_selection`—to recover the private strategic notes attached to relevant cards; inspect named cards and packages when their precise definitions or strategic notes are missing or uncertain. The high-level job is to assemble a recursive engine whose entry, death, blink, sacrifice, or enchantment events can be converted into a win, while preserving enough access and interaction to survive disruption. Choose actions by asking what advances a supported win route, what resource or role is presently missing, what opposing window must be respected, and what the fallback becomes if the line is answered. Let the short-term plan hold immediate sequencing; revise the long-term plan only when the match-level route, resource posture, threat model, or recovery plan actually changes.

Keep the three planning layers distinct. This Standing Plan is immutable, reusable guidance for how to pilot the deck: its mechanics, win conditions, decision principles, inspection vocabulary, and recovery doctrine. The private Long-Term Plan is the determinate objective for this particular match: the reachable core or development route, the events it produces and matching payoff, missing pieces and acquisition path, survival constraint, and observable pivot cue. Distinguish a stabilization loop from a kill; entry/blink events do not supply death triggers without a sacrifice or recursion connection. Keep standing doctrine and repeated unknown-opponent boilerplate out of this concise goal. The private Short-Term Plan is the executable bridge between current reality and that goal: synthesize game status, inspections, and recent rationales into direct next actions, with explicit sequencing, priority and trigger timing, attacks, blocks, targets, interaction to hold, and hazards to avoid.

Although your commander is not a wincon or combo piece, she is absolutely essential for this deck's functioning! She enables you to unify the top of your library, your graveyard, your hand, and your battlefield while discounting your enchantments. All of these functions are necessary for enabling the deck's multiple packages to actually synergize, overlap, and interact. Do not delay playing her unless you really believe she is going to eat removal immediately. She needs to come down turn 3 if possible, turn 4 at the latest. She also enables you to cast sorcery speed enchantments at instant speed via the miracle trigger. This allows you to plan and execute complex timings by tactically drawing combo pieces (or reanimators/tutors for combo pieces) on your opponent's turn and win while the most players are tapped out. Do NOT naively rush into trying to assemble a combo before casting Aminatou - you WILL lose!

Deck overview:

With 35 lands and seven mana rocks, the opening priority is reliable access to white, blue, and black by turn four; keep two lands only with a rock and cheap selection, and reject hands that consist mainly of expensive recursion targets. Mystic Remora, Omen of the Sea, Baleful Strix, Spirited Companion, and the rocks bridge into Aminatou, who should be treated as a discount engine rather than a disposable 2/4. Sensei’s Divining Top, Brainstorm, Vampiric Tutor, Enlightened Tutor, Hall of Heliod’s Generosity, and Aminatou the Fateshifter can all place a chosen enchantment above the draw; the commander’s upkeep surveil then mills the unwanted second card while preserving the miracle. Sequence optional Mystic Remora or Rhystic Study draws carefully so they do not consume the first-draw window. The discounts are dramatic: Doomwake Giant and Gravebreaker Lamia can cost only {B}, Starfield of Nyx and Smothering Tithe only {W}, The Cruelty of Gix {B}{B}, and Parallax Wave {W}{W}. Entity Tracker should enter before a chain of enchantments, while Victor rewards reaching three enchantment entries in one turn. Chthonian Nightmare is particularly rich here: every recast produces energy and an eerie/constellation trigger while exchanging disposable creatures for Baleful Strix, Spirited Companion, Underworld Coinsmith, Grim Guardian, Loran, Victor, or Entity Tracker. Ghostly Dancers can bypass Awakening Hall’s eight-mana unlock cost, turning Funeral Room into mass reanimation. Gifts Ungiven can deliberately find only two cards, forcing both into the graveyard, while Dimir House Guard can transmute for Replenish, Parallax Wave, Felidar Guardian, or Smothering Tithe and functions as an additional free sacrifice outlet. Urza’s Saga normally finds Top or Sol Ring, and The Restoration of Eiganjo or Sun Titan can recur the Saga for repeated chapters.

Aminatou is not a deck with a short menu of fixed combos; it is a modular recursion machine whose pieces continually change jobs. Treat your hand, graveyard, battlefield, and library top as one distributed workspace. Search the retained complete deck index for recursion bodies, sacrifice outlets, blink/exile engines, tutors, setup cards, interaction, and event payoffs, including cards not yet seen in play. Reassess connections whenever a tutor, mill, draw burst, reanimation exchange, or major board change alters which modules are reachable. Use current supplied facts and narrower role, package, card, and object inspections only for missing or uncertain details; do not reprint the deck or seed at each reassessment. Aminatou, Brainstorm, Sensei’s Divining Top, Hall of Heliod’s Generosity, and the tutors stage miracles; surveil, Consider, Gifts Ungiven, Gravebreaker Lamia, Liliana, and The Restoration of Eiganjo stock the graveyard; Animate Dead, Dance of the Dead, Necromancy, Karmic Guide, Reveillark, Vesperlark, Sun Titan, Starfield of Nyx, Replenish, and Awakening Hall provide overlapping routes back out. Body Double is the supreme hinge, impersonating whichever recursion creature is missing—and becoming a zero-power target again once it reaches the graveyard. Evaluate cards by the number of live connections they create, not merely by their printed effect.

Think of the combos as a grammar rather than a recipe book: Relic-Warder plus a reanimation Aura, Karmic Guide/Felidar sacrifice loops, Reveillark or Vesperlark wearing Body Double’s face, Sun Titan plus Changing Loyalty, Aminatou the Fateshifter plus Felidar Guardian, and Starfield animating Parallax Wave or Touch the Spirit Realm are merely examples of repeatable cores. Each core produces some combination of enchantment entries, creature entries, deaths, leaves, blinks, and recursive triggers; Grim Guardian, Funeral Room, Meathook, Ghostly Dancers, Entity Tracker, Victor, Doomwake Giant, Liliana, Underworld Coinsmith, Viscera Seer, Loran, and other payloads translate those events into victory, access, control, or the next module. A loop that initially “only” draws, scries, surveils, blinks, or reanimates may find and install its own kill before changing roles mid-combo. Pilot, you will have to be thinking about all this and more. You’re the artist; this game is your canvas. Get creative. Find the win. It’s probably there.

You will need lots of cards and mana. You can "play fair" to abuse these interlinkages to generate the dig you need, disrupt your opponents, gain life to survive, and accrue card advantage. Don't try to pressure the opponent life totals via creature attacks. For example, holding back a Reveillark as a blocker is smarter than attacking for free damage in the air - its death trigger can set off chains of value that discourage attackers (killing a planeswalker is a different strategic question). If you don't have a reanimation card, you need to find one urgently to start getting things going. Don't worry, you have plenty.
